The human moment

A Universal Credit claimant asks for an advance.

  1. A model flags the request
  2. A caseworker decides, not told a model made the referral
  3. The claimant appeals
  4. Months later, a tribunal

The review that demonstrably changes outcomes comes later.

Why it matters now

Since February 2026 the law turns on whether human involvement is “meaningful”.

The Information Commissioner must now prepare a code of practice on AI and automated decision-making. It has not yet been written.

Questions people ask before reading

Is this vendor material?

No. Every figure traces to a department’s own published record, the National Audit Office, the Public Accounts Committee, the Ministry of Justice, a tribunal, the Information Commissioner’s Office or the statute book. No vendor source appears anywhere in it.

Is it free to download?

Yes. It downloads directly, with no form and no email address required.

Is the briefing critical of DWP and the Home Office?

It uses only what each department has published, what auditors and Parliament have recorded, and tribunal findings. DWP publishes an annual assessment of its model, which is more than most organisations do, and the briefing says so. The argument is about what is not yet measured, the human review, not about bad faith.

Which parts of the UK does it cover?

DWP’s Universal Credit decisions cover Great Britain; Northern Ireland runs its own system through the Department for Communities, and Scotland’s devolved benefits sit with Social Security Scotland. Home Office asylum decisions are UK-wide.

How current is the research?

Verified against primary sources as at 29 September 2026, including DWP’s assessment of 9 July 2026, the NAO’s May 2026 good practice guide, the Ministry of Justice’s tribunal statistics of 10 September 2026 and the GOV.UK transparency register on 29 September 2026.

What if I want to take this further?

The briefing ends with an eight-workstream action plan. The first three, readiness assessment, use case triage and a regulatory and legal position paper, form a natural first engagement of six to eight weeks, enough to know whether a department’s review would stand up to the ICO’s coming code.
