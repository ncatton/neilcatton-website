The Next Evolution
Review

A periodical. Every two months, one question is given the room a single essay never has. Free to read, sourced throughout, and nothing in it is a reprint.

Why a periodical

An argument that takes six pieces

Between July and September 2026, four essays on {{substackName}} made versions of the same argument. The subjects were different and so were the sectors. The point underneath was the same, and the repetition went unrecognised until the fourth.

That is the cost of publishing several times a week. Each piece stands alone, and a reader who arrives at the fourth has no way of knowing the first three exist. An argument that takes six pieces to build is never visible in any one of them.

The Review is the slower form. Each issue takes one question, sets a long cover story against it, and gathers shorter articles that come at the same question from other sectors. Where an argument first appeared in shorter form on the Substack, the fact box at the foot of the article names the original.

How it is built

What every issue carries

Cases where the argument fails

Each issue includes cases its own argument does not explain, and grades them in the open. Issue One has three.

A source for every factual claim

Numbered notes in each article's fact box point to the primary source. Judgements are labelled as judgements.

A corrections page

It appears in every issue, including when it is empty. Issue One carries a correction to an essay published in September.

Dated calls

Forecasts with a stated likelihood and a date. Each is scored in the first issue after its date passes, right or wrong.

A three-line summary on every article

The case, the claim and what it costs, so the whole issue can be skimmed in a few minutes.

The data behind the figures

The ledger of cases and the calls are published as CSV beside each issue, so the arithmetic can be checked.

The method page in each issue says how the cases were chosen, what the author declares, and how AI assistance was used in the drafting.

If something is wrong, say so

Corrections are published in the next issue, with the reader credited unless they ask otherwise, and the original is amended with a dated note.

Send a correction

Quote it, cite it, send it on

Attribution is the only condition. The Review is published every two months and is free. There is no subscriber target, and no brief set by anyone other than the author. New issues are announced on the Substack.
