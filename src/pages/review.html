How it is built

What every issue carries

  • Cases where the argument failsEach issue includes cases its own argument does not explain, and grades them in the open. Issue One has three.
  • A source for every factual claimNumbered notes in each article’s fact box point to the primary source. Judgements are labelled as judgements.
  • A corrections pageIt appears in every issue, including when it is empty. Issue One carries a correction to an essay published in September.
  • Dated callsForecasts with a stated likelihood and a date. Each is scored in the first issue after its date passes, right or wrong.
  • A three-line summary on every articleThe case, the claim and what it costs, so the whole issue can be skimmed in a few minutes.
  • The data behind the figuresThe ledger of cases and the calls are published as CSV beside each issue, so the arithmetic can be checked.

The method page in each issue says how the cases were chosen, what the author declares, and how AI assistance was used in the drafting.

Why a periodical

An argument that takes six pieces to build is never visible in any one of them.

The longer version

Between July and September 2026, four essays on The Next Evolution made versions of the same argument. The subjects were different and so were the sectors. The point underneath was the same, and the repetition went unrecognised until the fourth.

That is the cost of publishing several times a week. Each piece stands alone, and a reader who arrives at the fourth has no way of knowing the first three exist.

The Review is the slower form. Each issue takes one question, sets a long cover story against it, and gathers shorter articles that come at the same question from other sectors. Where an argument first appeared in shorter form on the Substack, the fact box at the foot of the article names the original.
