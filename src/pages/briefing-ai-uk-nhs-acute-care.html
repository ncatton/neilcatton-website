AI in NHS Acute Care: Patient Flow and Triage

Every patient in acute care is already ranked by a score or an algorithm. The score misses some patients who are getting worse, and their families catch them. AI is now arriving at every point where patients are ranked.

Why this briefing

NHS England records, for every Martha's Rule call about a patient getting worse, whether the early warning score would have raised the alarm on its own. Between June 2025 and August 2026, in 83 per cent of those calls where a score had been taken, it would not have. In 69 per cent of those cases the patient then needed a change in treatment or a transfer of care. Seven in ten Martha's Rule calls come from families and carers.

AI is now arriving at every point where patients are ranked. AI triage in the NHS App is due to reach every App user by April 2028; the evidence given for it is a 29 per cent cut in the phone queue at one practice, where an NIHR-funded evaluation runs until August 2027. An AI tool already forecasts A&E demand for 50 NHS organisations. Nothing published measures who these tools miss.

The rules are being written now. The government has accepted all 44 recommendations of the National Commission on regulating AI in healthcare, and neither the Commission's report nor the response mentions triage. Meanwhile the clinical safety standards every trust works to sit under a power that changed in July 2025 from "have regard to" to "must comply", and now reaches IT suppliers directly.

The human moment

The patient the score did not flag, and the family who did. A woman on a ward is getting worse. Her observations are taken and the early warning score does not trigger. Her daughter can see something is wrong and uses Martha's Rule to ask for an urgent review. NHS England's own data shows that in most such calls the score had not raised the alarm, and that most of those patients then needed their care changed.

The discipline behind it

Every figure carries a source: NHS England's official statistics or its own published record, the Care Quality Commission, the Health Services Safety Investigations Body, a coroner's report, a government department or the statute book. Where the briefing reports an absence — such as the word "triage" in the National Commission's report — the source was searched for it.

The most quotable numbers in the sector are excluded on the record: a supplier's "94% triage accuracy" for AI triage, attributed to an NHS evaluation that has not been published, and NHS England's £41bn benefits estimate for its technology programme, published without a method. The people who died in the coroners' cases are described, not named.

Who it's for

A chief executive, medical director, chief clinical information officer or chief information officer at an acute trust who has just picked up an AI programme, or a board member asked whether the trust's scores and tools can be shown to catch the patients they should. It assumes acute care is well understood and AI is not.

Frequently asked

Questions people ask before reading

Is this vendor material?

No. Every figure traces to NHS England's official statistics or its own published record, a regulator, a safety investigator, a coroner's report, a government department or the statute book. No supplier performance figure is used, and a supplier's headline accuracy claim is named as excluded.

Is it free to download?

Yes. It downloads directly, with no form and no email address required.

Is the briefing critical of NHS England or the NHS App?

It uses only what NHS England and others have published. NHS England publishes Martha's Rule data monthly, which is more than most health systems do, and the briefing says so. The argument is about what is not yet measured — who a score or tool misses — not about anyone's good faith.

Is NHS Pathways artificial intelligence?

No. NHS Pathways, the triage system behind NHS 111 and more than half of England's 999 services, is rule-based clinical decision support. It appears because it is the baseline any AI triage tool will be compared with, and because coroners' reports show where triage failures surface.

Which parts of the UK does it cover?

England. Health is devolved, and the NHS bodies, statistics and standards here are England's. The MHRA, the data protection law and the National Commission on AI in healthcare are UK-wide.

How current is the research?

Verified against primary sources as at 15 October 2026, including NHS England's statistics published on 8 October 2026, the government's response to the National Commission of 6 October 2026, and the Health Bill's stage in Parliament on 15 October 2026.

What if I want to take this further?

The briefing ends with an eight-workstream action plan. The first three — readiness assessment, use case triage, and a regulatory and legal position paper — form a natural first engagement of six to eight weeks, enough to know whether a trust can show who its scores and tools miss.
